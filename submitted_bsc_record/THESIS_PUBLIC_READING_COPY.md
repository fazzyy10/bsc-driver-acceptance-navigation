## Abstract

Nowadays, more vehicles are getting manufactured, and more vehicles are in the road. People have already transformed into the age of vehicles, it can be personal vehicles or public transport, but people rely on vehicles over any traditional method for means of transport more than ever. And with the rise of consummation of vehicles, the increase in road accidents has become inevitable. There are so many fatalities and property damages happen in all over the world because of road accidents. Therefore, mitigating road accidents has become a global objective and most IT companies and technological studies are specifically focusing on it. As such, according to past researches and experts of technology field, believe an efficient navigation system could assist drivers to perform safe driving, so they make informed decisions while driving and make sure accidents won’t occur.  With the technological advancements and digitization, these navigation systems are directly available through mobiles. Therefore, drivers can really access mobile navigation systems and make sure they don’t drive recklessly, or they don’t come across an unexpected situation while driving.

Hence, we need to make sure that drivers utilize these mobile navigation systems to perform safe driving to avoid accidents. This is where the driver acceptance of mobile navigation systems comes in to the picture. Many renowned authors believe if we can increase the driver acceptance in mobile navigation systems, more and more drivers would utilize mobile navigation systems to perform safe driving where road accidents would be mitigated in great numbers. Especially in Sri Lanka, past studies have proven that if the drivers have prior information about road conditions, traffic congestion information, changing weather patterns and light condition information, road accidents will be mitigated in great margins. The major solution of acquiring all these information from one source is mobile navigation systems. Therefore, it is important that we make sure drivers do utilize mobile navigation systems in order to mitigate road accidents in Sri Lanka. Therefore, the major purpose of this research is to recognize significant factors which could impact driver acceptance in mobile navigation systems. After identifying these factors, mobile navigation systems service providers can manipulate these variables by making changes in their products and processes to increase driver acceptance rate in mobile navigation systems which could eventually mitigate road accidents in Sri Lanka.

First variables for the research will be identified through past literature, and variables will be measured through quantitative data collection which will consist of drivers’ perceptions. Then collected data are analyzed and variables measured to identify the significant variables influence driver acceptance of mobile navigation systems. Finally, findings and recommendations are presented, and limitations will be discussed which would be beneficial for future research.


## Introduction


### Research Background

In present, Road accidents are a bothersome phenomenon all around the world which is highly correlated with the health of people in a country, and it directly influence the mortality rates in countries. The increasing trend of road accidents could cause socio and economic issues because of loss of life and damage to property. Without proper discipline of the drivers, pedestrians and without appropriate prior information about roadway conditions, road accidents are really induced.

World Health Organization  states that 1.35 Million fatalities and 50 Million more injuries to people, occur every year due to road accidents, making road accidents a leading circumstance of death globally. Sure enough, WHO has concluded that road accidents are presently estimated as the 7th leading reason of fatality amongst all age groups internationally and predicted to become the 9th leading reason of fatality by the year 2030 and this rise will be driven by low- and middle-income countries. In a business perspective, the statistics concludes that in most nations the cost of road accidents accounts for up to 3% of a country’s GDP and more than ninety percent of these accidents happen in low- and middle-income countries with lower socio-economic standards.

In Sri Lanka as elsewhere, road accidents are a serious problem, World bank  states, annually 38,000 road accidents occur in Sri Lanka which is the result of approximately 3,000 fatalities and 8,000 major injuries. Sri Lanka’s expected annual road accidents fatalities per capita is the highest across Sri Lanka’s immediate neighbors in South Asia and five times that of the best performing nations across the globe. 1 in 45 fatalities is occurred due to road accidents in Sri Lanka and the previous trends in accidents tell that the risk will be doubled for the next generation.

These accidents impact the development of the country and cause huge losses to the economy. The World Bank  reports, the estimated economic cost of road accidents in Sri Lanka is $4,001 Million as of  2016 which is 4.9% of the country’s total GDP. Moreover, World Bank forecasts, in the future, these road accidents may cost nations such as Sri Lanka from 3 to 5% of the annual GDP. Moreover, As per world bank, Sri Lanka will need an extra investment of around $2 billion in the upcoming 10 years if Sri Lanka to reach expected SDGs (Sustainable Development Goals) of 3.6 target of a 50% cutback in road accident deaths.

This is a problem which is important to the nation’s public health, wellbeing, human capital, and economic and social development. Hence, above numbers encourage to mitigate road accidents in any means.

As far as mitigation of road accidents is concerned, drivers play a crucial part as always. According to Adell, E , the world has seen a healthy development of different driver support systems to mitigate road accidents. For example, “Traffic Management Systems, Navigation Information Systems, Intelligent Speed Adaptation Systems, Automotive Collision Avoidance Systems, Fatigue Monitoring Systems, Road-Departure Crash Warning Systems” are some of the present systems in use. If these systems, are to be successful in mitigating road accidents, these systems must be utilized by the drivers. For this driver acceptance and adoption of the navigation systems is most important.

Najm, et al  concludes in his paper of “Evaluation of an Automotive Rear-End Collision Avoidance System” that, “driver acceptance is the precondition that will permit new automotive technologies to achieve their forecasted benefit levels” and Van der Laan, et al.  states “it is unproductive to invest effort in designing and building an intelligent co-driver if the system is never switched on, or even disabled”. Hence it is the driver who make the choice whether to use or not a use a navigation system. Therefore, driver support systems should be more persuasive and effective, so the driver is drawn to use these systems in order to ensure road safety.

Danthanarayana & Mallikahewa  has conducted a research about the “enduring factors of road traffic accidents in Sri Lanka” and their statistical model reveal that when the number of operated vehicles and road length increase, so does the road accidents. Therefore, informing drivers about road lengths of their journeys and how many vehicles are operated in a road while they are driving is vital to mitigate road accidents whereas these information are provided by most of the mobile navigation systems such as google maps. As per Google , “Google maps Roads” API enables drivers to map GPS coordinates to the geometry of the relevant road and to understand speed limitations towards respective roads.

According to Dhananjaya & Alibuhtto  research on “factors influencing road accidents in Sri Lanka: A logistic regression approach”, they have identified following factors to be significant in terms of road accidents in Sri Lanka (from 2010-2014 police records data).

- Traffic Congestion
- Weather Condition
- Location Type (4-leg junction, T-junction, Stretch road)
- Road Surface Condition
- Light Condition
Dhananjaya & Alibuhtto conclude that when the driver has above information about above variables in real time, or awared on beforehand, road accidents could be vastly mitigated. And all these information are provided real time in mobile navigation systems nowadays with the use of GPS and variety of other technologies. For example, according to Google , google maps is gaining “safe routing” option to help avoid accidents which factors in the present conditions of the roads, weather, and traffic. According to Sundar Pichai, Google’s objective for “safe routing” in Google Maps is to decrease the amount of road accidents annually by as many as hundred million.

The only problem is whether the drivers are utilizing these moblie navigation systems in order to prevent road accidents. Therefore increasing the user acceptance of mobile navigation systems is most important in mitigating road accidents. Especially when it comes to Mobile Navigation Information Systems, Aleska & Kaiser  states, within the last decade, mobile navigation information systems have become the widely spread user component in terms of easy transportation and road safety. According to Feenstra, et al. , mobile navigation systems, could sophisticate drivers with real time, correct road condition information to mitigate road accidents, transportation routes which could be essential in unfamiliar routes and in a comprehensive road network which has traffic congestion.

Moreover Lee & Cheng , concludes that, when it comes to driver performance, and vehicle control, mobile navigation systems are far more effective compared to in-vehicle navigation on urban and rural roads and it is much fuel-efficient in unusual traffic situations. The penetration of mobile devices in Sri Lanka has increased exponentially in recent times and everyone possesses a mobile device these days, therefore the accessibility of mobile navigation systems is too easy where as in-vehicle navigation systems are only accessible for drivers mostly with cars and vans.

Adell, E , emphasizes mobile navigation applications are more attractive to drivers, which associated with the low cost of mobile navigation applications, convenient update methods, and high precision. Likewise mobile navigation systems provides more advantages to the driver and to the road safety and for these reasons drivers should be encouraged to use mobile navigation systems. Therefore, identifying factors which could influence driver acceptance of mobile navigation information systems is crucial in terms of mitigating road accident in Sri Lanka.


### Research Problem

As mentioned earlier, driver acceptance of mobile navigation systems is vital in mitigating road accidents. Hence, the research problem is identified as to recognize which factors could influence driver acceptance in mobile navigation systems significantly and find ways to increase driver acceptance by manipulating these factors in order to mitigate road accidents in Sri Lanka.

Drivers’ perceptions are known to be an important element of these systems as mobile navigation systems are generally evaluated in terms of driver experience and the accuracy of displayed geographic information of the system. According to Kawasaki, et al. , these two dimensions are widely known as the psychological characteristics of the systems. Even though these two psychological characteristics contribute to the improvement of mobile navigation systems which gives greater services to users, low number of researchers only have researched driver perspectives in regard to the use of mobile navigation systems.

May be this is because of past studies have only researched about the engineering development of technologies in mobile navigation systems or these systems contain variety of diverse technologies. Since navigation systems could be easily consumed by any IOT device in present, a study of drivers’ perceptions who interact with these mobile navigation systems must be considered to be a psychological perspective worth studying than only giving attention on the hardware and technical perceptions of system engineers. Not only the psychological factors of mobile navigation systems, but also the functional expectancy that highly affect drivers’ perceptions toward the system acceptance must be studied to provide valuable user interface information.

Moreover, most scholars focuses on mobile navigation systems’ reliability and convenience. Wickens. Not only distraction, when it comes to navigation information systems, Adell, E  concludes, the lack of real time information, reliability of the system, user friendliness, social influence and security issues are considered as critical factors which influence in usability of these systems. Mobile navigations systems should be assisting the driver without causing much distraction and providing useful information simultaneously. Hence, to provide insights on improving overall performance of mobile navigation systems also is considered as another objective of this research.

## Literature Review


### 2.1 Introduction to Mobile Navigation Systems

According to Narzt, et al  and Schiller & Voisard , mobile navigation systems contain three basic technical components which provide drivers with navigation services. First technical component is a trustworthy road map database, with enough data storage and consist of accurate GPS location data like building names and road numbers. The second component is navigation systems must provide a display and a visualization feature.

Most of mobile navigation systems provide a rotational synchronizing technique with a bird-eye viewpoint to show  their GPS map for the drivers’ sophistication. Apart from visual aid, Representations of distance measurement and feedback using sound and visual effects are also equally important. These solutions must  embed with mobile devices because they provide spatial data and communicate with drivers. Third major technical component is “system integration” and “real-time synchronization” for receiving and displaying the appropriate information in precise moments. , , , . These three components are considered fundamental features of a navigation system since the initial introduction of the commercial car navigation system by Steve Lobbezoo.

In present, the navigation systems incorporated in vehicles contain in-vehicle navigation systems such as “FlyAudio” or “CASKA” or etc. Examples for portable navigation devices are “Garmin”, “TomTom”, etc. Moreover, example for mobile navigation applications are “Google Maps”, “Gaode Maps” and “Baidu Maps” . Zheng,Y  reports, a survey concludes, 49.6% and 22.1% drivers utilize mobile navigation applications and the other two navigation systems, respectively.  Moreover, according to Canalys , 7.4 million mobile navigation devices shipped in Q2 2007, up 116% on Q2 2006 and Worldwide mobile navigation device market more than doubled.


### 2.2 Driver Acceptance of Mobile Navigation Systems

In general, user acceptance of certain systems is a popular research topic in any industry. There are so many theoretical studies had been carried out to understand proper measurements of user perceptions of specific systems. The “Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)”, introduced by Davis, is one of the most popular, vastly applied, and successful methodologies in explaining how users accept a specific system and develop individualistic perspectives on those particular systems.  &

So many past studies of mobile technologies and GIS systems have applied TAM as the primary model to elucidate users’ perspectives. For instance, TAM conceptual model has been applied to analyze “users’ acceptance of mobile guide services” , “mobile tourism services” , and “mobile map services” . Furthermore, TAM has been applied in variety of researches which tried to explore the workflow by which users employ specific technologies in variety of fields like systems for assisting drivers  and in-car user interfaces . Since TAM model enables to analyze user acceptance of specific systems by providing a comprehensive framework, this research will apply TAM model as the primary framework for a new integrated theoretical model to analyze factors which influence driver acceptance of mobile navigation systems.

When it comes to measuring user acceptance, Davis , the most important dimension to look at is the “intention to use”. By measuring the intention to use, we can define user acceptance of a system more vividly. Moreover, Yang  and fellow researches also used drivers’ “intention to use” as a suitable dimension to define driver’s acceptance of  mobile navigation applications. Finally, Park & Kim  also acknowledge applying intention to use as a better dimension to measure driver’s acceptance. They break down the intention of a driver into two distinct categories such as intention to use and intention to recommend. These dimensions can be used to define driver acceptance of mobile navigations systems in a much better way.


### 2.3 The Original TAM Model and Its Main Constructs

According to TAM model, there are variety of studies which have been carried out to measure user acceptance of navigation products. Chen & Chen  have analyzed the contributing factors that influence “travelers’ usage intentions of GPS devices” and assessed the moderating effects of individual creativeness on the connection of attitude and behavior intention. Park, et al. , in their paper, “Driver acceptance of car navigation systems: integration of locational accuracy, processing speed, and service and display quality with technology acceptance model”, founded a new theoretical model which infused TAM model with several other characteristics like “perceived locational accuracy”, “service and display quality” and “perceived processing speed” to analyze driver acceptance in car navigation systems.

Xu, et al.  introduced another extended model of Technology Acceptance Model that contained “information attributes, travel information trust, socio demographics and alternate route cognition” to analyze “the acceptance of advanced traveler information systems”. Park, et al.  investigated how TAM could be applied to predict mobile map services users’ perceptions toward the map service, the outcome of the study emphasized factors such as “perceived mobility” and “perceived locational accuracy” predominantly contributed to user acceptance variance and intention to use mobile map services.

The above literature reveals, past researches introduced variety of their own extended TAM models to navigation products and users of these products divulged the contribution of the new factors on intention to use these systems. But all these past literature have mostly focused on user acceptance of “in-vehicle navigation systems” than on “mobile navigation systems”. Mobile navigation systems have variety of characteristics compared to in-vehicle navigation systems, like a smaller screen and real time notifications. Hence, researches carried out on in-vehicle navigation systems wont’ completely emphasize the user acceptance of mobile navigation systems. Consistent with prior research in user acceptance and considering Technology Acceptance Model as the primary framework, this study proposes a driver acceptance model that has the two main components of the original TAM which are “perceived usefulness” and “perceived ease of use”.

According to Park & Kim  perceived usefulness can be measured by dimensions such compatibility, convenience, simplicity, and availability whereas perceived usefulness can be measured by dimensions such as sense of usefulness, effectiveness, safety, and fitness for use. These dimensions are acknowledged by Yang, et al  and his fellow researchers as well in their study of driver acceptance in car navigation systems.

Moreover, this research will employ another few characteristics that could influence user acceptance and driver perceptions in regard to mobile navigation systems. In the following, the literature will explain of how the other characteristics are chosen and how their effects can be elucidated on drivers’ perceptions of mobile navigation systems.


### 2.4 Perceived Locational Accuracy (PLA)

When it comes to navigation systems which provide GPS, spatial and locational data is known as one of the most essential characteristics of these systems. Past literature have explained perceived locational accuracy as “the degree to which users of geographic information services are becoming aware of their current locations”  & . Park & Kim  again redefined perceived locational accuracy as “the degree to which drivers of automobiles are becoming aware of their accurate current locations and related environments”. Further they identified perceived locational accuracy as a key determinant to impact driver perceptions towards car navigation systems.

In mobile navigation systems, drivers could be able to find out their real time location and directions to desired destinations in bird eye view electronic map and arrow typed symbol. And the mobile navigation system must assist the driver with precise directions that prompt the drivers to the ultimate destinations effectively whereas this interaction should happen while driving real time. Hence, the greater the efficacy and spatial accuracy of mobile navigation systems’ locations, the more drivers are likely to accept it. Considering these reasons, this research proposes “Perceived locational accuracy” factor should be analyzed to check whether it is significantly related to acceptance of mobile navigation systems. Considering past literature such Park & Kim’s  research in “Driver acceptance of car navigation systems”, “Perceived Locational Accuracy” is measured in dimensions such as efficiency and accuracy. Moreover, they applied elements such as Information efficiency, general location accuracy and real time information accuracy for measurement purposes.


### 2.5 Perceived Processing Speed (PPS)

”Perceived Processing Speed” term is initially defined by Park and Kim  as “the users’ perception of processing speed of a particular system or technology”. Some past researches also revealed that the quality of a specific system or product could be strongly determined by the processing speed (and they identified perceived processing speed as a key determinant to influence driver perceptions towards car navigation systems through their research). For example, Buss  emphasize that users’ perspectives across a provided system or product that is significantly connected with the “perceived processing speed” of the respective system. Pagani  revealed, users’ intentions of the adoption of data services are positively impacted by the data speed of the relevant services.

According to these past literature, one can assume a higher processing speed is more likely to motivate drivers to accept mobile navigation systems. Therefore, analyzing the relationship between perceived processing speed and driver acceptance of mobile navigation systems is crucial in terms of significance of the proposed model. According to past literature such Park & Kim’s , Perceived Processing Speed (PPS) is measured in dimensions such as data rapidity and service urgency in responses. And the elements they used to measure perceived processing speed are loading and displaying speed, rapid processing advantage and prompt responses.


### 2.6 Service and Display Quality (SDQ)

The term “service quality” was initially defined by DeLone & McLean  as “the degree of overall performance of an information service and system”. Past researches of Cheong & Park , Ahn, et al.  and Wixom & Todd  have concluded that service quality of a system positively impact users’ determination to use the system. For instance, Lin & Hsieh  revealed “perceived service quality of self-service technologies significantly affects users’ behavioral intentions to employ those technologies”. Moreover, Ahn, et al.  his fellow researchers, determined that “perceived service quality” is an essential factors of users’ “technology acceptance of internet services”.

Even though mobile navigation systems, contain service and display components, those must be taken to a single construct. Researchers of visual technologies like Kim, et al.  revealed that viewing quality positively influence users’ perceived usability of an information system. According to past literature such as Park & Kim’s , Perceived Processing Speed (PPS) is measured in dimensions such as sufficiency, fitness for purpose and understandability. They measured this variable through these dimensions and identified service and display quality as a key determinant to influence driver perceptions towards car navigation systems. To measure this variable, they used elements such as clear information display, ability of displayed information to meet expectations and fitness for daily tasks.


### Distraction Perception

Yang, et al.  and his colleagues define the term “distraction perception” as “the perceived effects of distraction caused by navigation applications on individuals’ vision, hearing, decision-making and behaviors”. Navigation systems are most helpful for drivers in transportation, but they do cause variety of negative effects on driving. According to Jamson , a study revealed that 10% to 30% of drivers told, they occasionally enter or change a destination while they drive. Lansdown  conducted a research to determine distractions occurred by destination entry and the outcome of the study revealed, the participants rated their distractions as “medium”.

Of course, destination entry is a work which requires visual and movement attributes of drivers whereas driving also requires the same resources. Hurts, et al.  reports, visual-manual distraction is significantly connected with 5–25% of all road accidents, and some scholars think, this value is as larger as 80%. Apart of destination entry, the receiving of navigation data also could be a distraction to drivers. Morris, et al.  carried out a research understand the glance behaviors of drivers when interacting with a navigation device and a green-driving advisory device and concluded that drivers’ glances at personal navigation device account for the majority of the increase in eyes-off-road time. Hernández, et al. and his fellow colleagues revealed that when drivers continuously interact with navigation systems, the attention and focus paid to external road information significantly decreased.

For example, because a driver is kept glancing at their personal navigation system, traffic signs and overhead panels of the road could be missed to notice. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, US  reveal that, if a driver couldn’t pay attention to the road beyond two seconds, it will significantly increase road accidents and damage to property. And Finally, Yang. L and his fellow researchers  has identified distraction perception is one of the significant factors which affects the driver’s perception to use mobile navigation systems.

Therefore, above literature confirms that, the interactions with navigation systems while driving cause significant distractions and these distractions could easily cause a road crash or create traffic congestion. But, whether the distraction perception construct would prevent drivers’ from accepting mobile navigations systems remains to be analyzed in Sri Lanka context because past researches haven’t analyzed this relationship.


### Satisfaction

In their studies, Wixom & Todd  and  have agreed with the concept that satisfaction in using information systems is strongly connected with the “perceived usability” of a system. Park & Kim  also revealed that  satisfaction in using data sharing services is a significant factor of users’ behavioral intention to use the services. Further more, Park & del Pobil , stated users’ perceived satisfaction is one of the important variable influencing the users’ intention to use interactive devices. According to these literature, it is pretty evident, satisfaction could be a factor, where drivers look at before accepting a mobile navigation system. Therefore, the relationship between satisfaction and user acceptance of mobile navigation systems should be analyzed. According to past literature such as Park & del Pobil’s , satisfaction is measured in dimensions such as fitness for user expectations, Interactivity, and willingness to recommend.


### Social Influence

Social influence refers to the “contribution of external social factors of user acceptance”. Venkatesh, et al.  introduced the “Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology” (UTAUT) which is an extended version of TAM and defined the term “social influence” in the context of user acceptance as “the degree to which a user thinks others whether the user should use a particular system”. In the UTAUT model, Venkatesh concludes, persuasive social information changes user perceptions towards usefulness of new systems. In general, social impact is one of the major factors which could influence any person’s perception towards any system. We always draw ourselves to what majority is already doing. Duflo, et al  stated that, to accept and adopt new technologies in developing countries such as Kenya, the extent to which people could be able to learn from one another is essential. Since Sri Lanka also a developing country it is important that social influence is to be taken as a serious variable in order to understand the driver’s acceptance of mobile navigation systems.


### Trust

Rotter  explains trust to be “an expectancy that the promise of an individual or group can be relied upon”. Following to it Bélanger & Carter  expand the definition of trust to “an individual’s overall willingness to put his or her confidence in others”. In this research, trust indicates to the confidence the drivers puts on mobile navigation systems. Driver perceives the mobile navigation system they intend to use is trustworthy, if the driver doesn’t trust the system, there would be no point. Moreover, mobile services are predominantly managing personal data of users, because of the personalization and context-awareness of the respective services. Functions of complicated systems are hard to understand. Context-aware services might contain unpredictable variables which the users might have the ability to assess. Mobile service networks are becoming complicated every day, and users might not get a grasp on with whom they are transacting or interacting. These concerns, raise trust as a user acceptance factor . Moreover, trust was introduced as an additional acceptance factor for mobile services by Kindberg, et al.  and Barnes & Huff . Therefore, according to past literature and rationale, taking trust into account is critical in terms of user acceptance of mobile navigation systems.

First, Participants are asked to provide their consent in participating in the pilot survey, secondly participants are requested to provide some demographic information about themselves such as whether they are acquired their driving license or not, age and how many years they have been driving. Age information is collected to mainly identify in which age group, mobile navigations systems are widely used. Since having driver’s license, is a mandatory qualification to participate in this survey, it is also asked. However, Author estimates, even though participants are people who, acquired their driver’s license, even among them, there could be participants who have acquired their driver’s license, but don’t really drive. And the author expect this fact would be proven from the final demographic question where participants are asked to answer that how many years have participants been driving. Moreover, it is expected, this question would provide more sense on participants’ driving experience and maturity levels in driving.

Demographic questions are presented to participants as multiple-choice questions and demographic information is only used to understand the participant sample better and is not considered for the analysis related to the main variables indicated in the study. And some past studies have included education, car navigation experience and gender as variables in their research too. As per Glen , a data sample with an alpha below 0.70 is questionable and not reliable whereas anything greater than 0.7 alpha is acceptable. Even if the Cronbach’s alpha is above 0.70 and if omitting any question could improve Cronbach’s alpha, the deletion of that question would be contemplated by taking the importance of the question into consideration in measuring the relevant variable.

Normally correlation coefficients are not easy to calculate however, with the statistical programs such as SPSS and RStudio, it has become pretty easy to find the coefficient.

#### 4.7.3 Multiple Linear Regression Analysis

Simple Linear regression is a tool where the relationship between one independent variable and one dependent variable is analyzed. And the data set should be linear whereas simple linear regression doesn’t really fit non-linear data sets. This is where multiple regression comes into the picture. Multiple regression is a concept where the relationship between one single dependent variable and multiple independent variables are analyzed.


*Figure 94: Multiple Regression Explanation (original figure/image may not be included in this text copy)*

Multiple regression is the extended version of linear regression where it allows more items into the linear regression equation with each item representing a significant influence on the dependent variable.

According to Hayes , Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) is a form multiple regression where linear relationship between a dependent variables and multiple independent variables are analyzed. The multiple regression model which has been performed in this study has several assumptions.

- The relationship among the dependent variable and the independent variables is linear not quadradic. (This was already proven in the correlation analysis)
- The independent variables in the research are not too highly correlated with each other.
- Yi observations are selected independently and randomly from the data set.
- Residuals must be normally scattered with a mean of Zero and variance alpha.
The main purpose to perform a multiple linear regression in this study to observe how well all the independent variables in this research collectively define the dependent variable through the statistical model. This will be measured by the coefficient of determination (R2) statistical metric value where it will measure how much of the variation in results can be explained by the variation in the independent variables.

(R2) is normally ranged from zero to one, whereas zero signifies that the dependent variable can’t be predicted by any of the independent variables, and one signifies that the dependent variable is 100% explained by the relevant independent variables of the model.

Following is the hypothesis (1MLR) for the multiple linear regression which was mentioned already in chapter 3.9.3.

H0 – Driver’s acceptance is not affected by perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, perceived locational accuracy, perceived processing speed, service and display quality, distraction perception, satisfaction, social influence, and trust.

H1 – Driver’s acceptance is affected by perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, perceived locational accuracy, perceived processing speed, service and display quality, distraction perception, satisfaction, social influence, and trust.

Considering the correlation tests conducted above, it is proven that all the 9 independent variables did correlate with the dependent variable significantly. Therefore all 9 independent variables are included in the regression test without nothing getting omitted.

The performed multiple regression supported the assumption of the residuals being distributed normally. The screenshots for support this fact are included in Appendix 7.7

Looking at the model summary, author has deemed the regression model is indeed significant at 0.01 level as the significance alpha is <0.001. Therefore, the null hypothesis has been rejected and it is proven that driver’s acceptance is affected by perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, perceived locational accuracy, perceived processing speed, service and display quality, distraction perception, satisfaction, social influence, and trust with a 99% confidence interval. The F statistic of the model is 70.887 with a degree of freedom of 9. R2 value is 0.679 which indicates this statistical model can predict 67.9% of the dependent variable whereas the remaining 32.1% could be accounted for other factors which are considered in the research. No zero-correlation coefficient was found in the regression model which supports the fact once more that all the independent variables do affect the dependent variable collectively. All the other screenshots of the regression testing in included in Appendix 7.7.

The MLR Equation for Driver Acceptance

Driver Acceptance = 0.105 + 0.039*Perceived Ease of Use + 0.157*Perceived Usefulness – 0.008*Perceived Locational Accuracy + 0.098*Perceived Processing Speed + 0.070*Service and Display Quality + 0.032*Distraction Perception + 0.340*Satisfaction – 0.031*Social Influence + 0.299*Trust

Nevertheless, this is a primitive statistical model only whereas a more effective statistical model could be possible to formulate by running some more multiple regression models with different combinations of the independent variables. Generally, deleting more variables has a possibility to reduce the R2 but including variables considering significance and excluding variables which don’t have significance might increase the R2 too. But these fluctuations of R2 will solely depend on the data set of the research. And the future research could try quadradic and other nonlinear regression models in predicting the dependent variable to get a high R2 value. However, author has decided to go through with this statistical model because of the time constraint of the research. Tarzey  in the Europe union, 58% of organizations directly exchange data with users from other companies and/or consumers. In UK alone this number goes up to 65%. IAM has become more crucial than ever in present. Identity as a Service (IDaaS) is the future of IAM, Therefore considering IDaaS as an effective choice is wiser than creating a new IAM strategy or developing an existing one.

Auditing should be conducted to find root causes of data breaches. And audit process could be automated with SCIM complaint Rest APIs. As far as compliance is concerned, to protect and not disclose personal information of drivers stored in mobile navigation systems and their servers, it is important to comply with new data privacy regulations. Hence, service providers need to make sure, navigations systems adhere to Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022 (the “Act” or “PDPA”) in Sri Lanka. . Moreover, service providers can adhere to GDPR and CCPA compliance also if they want to make it in international platform. Even though GDPR compliance is not relevant, adhering to following regulations of GDPR will built trust in drivers and will provide confidence to drivers to use a mobile navigation system.

- Article 5.1.(f): Organizations are obliged to show that they have a lawful cause to withhold user’s identifiable information and companies must provide proof which facilitate the fact that they protect these data with high security.
- Article 21.1: Proof of consent is required when an organization collect identifiable personal information from an individual
- Article 12.1: Organizations are obliged to apply clear and simple language in privacy notices and must be straight forward about user consents.
- Article 33.1: When there is a data breach, the organization which responsible for the data collected, must inform the relevant individuals about the data breach within 72 hours
- Article 17.1: Users have the right to demand their personal information from companies to hand over to them and not to use again. (“Right to be Forgotten” regulation)
Especially when reviewing Article 12.1 of GDPR, it can be used not only in privacy notices but also in mobile navigations systems as a whole. Using clear and simple language in the whole navigation system would benefit in increasing perceived ease of use.

Business Continuity Planning (BCP) and Management should be implemented by mobile navigation systems service providers to assist drivers even in a pandemic like Corona to mitigate road accidents. Infrastructure as a Service, Disaster Recovery such as AWS IaaS, AWS DRS, Amazon EKS Kubernetes will be vital in BCP.

Importance and better implementation of CIA triad also recommended to protect drivers’ personal information. Moreover, achieving ISO 27000 certification standards also will be an important milestone to streamline processes and attract more drivers in using mobile navigation systems.

All these best practices will serve better in protecting personal information of the drivers and providing accurate information to drivers while it increases trust between the driver and the mobile navigation system. Further, in the context of providing accurate information, it would increase perceived locational accuracy as well.

Moreover, brand image, recognition and the reputation of service providers are general trust triggers when accepting a mobile navigation system. Hence, service providers should keen on these elements. Government endorsements of mobile navigation systems and usage recommendations and encouragements also may increase trust in drivers from various elements like security, loyalty, and dependence. These elements addresses the social influence variable as well. Hence, investing on these elements would increase trust and social influence. Anyhow, educating the users of mobile navigation systems about all these security aspects is also vital where drivers are made aware how they can make sure their information is protected.

As pointed out earlier, providing mobile navigation systems with minimal complexity will increase the perceived ease of use for drivers which is deemed to influence driver acceptance positively. User guidelines and manuals can help the drivers to easily understand the system and use it to its core purpose. To make sure drivers’ queries are attended proactively or in need of additional assistance, customer care services should be accessible and available. Even if the service providers want to implement all the mentioned technologies above, the most important question they need to ask themselves before product release or software updates is: Would my customer (driver) understand the system completely without much hassle?.

In the chapter 4.7.1.3, perceived locational accuracy identified as to influence driver acceptance positively. The need for accurate location information was expressed in multiple answers in question 16 of final survey as well. And when it comes to road accidents, providing accurate GPS information would mitigate road accidents where the driver is not confused or mislead with wrong information. Therefore, service providers need to make sure, whatever information displayed in their mobile navigation system is accurate. They can incorporate Geographic Information Systems (GIS) with mobile navigation systems to provide accurate real time and general GPS data. Specially GIS heat maps application would be pretty efficient in this process where it can show at what locations there are larger traffic congestions.

Big Data Analytics has enabled GIS to generate visuals and analyze on the fly, therefore mobile navigation systems can have the ability, generate time animations, and reveal spatiotemporal patterns in order to provide accurate locations.

Further, they can release their own small IoT device which combines its physical location from the real time GPS receiver giving real-time sensor information and positioning to the driver. Service providers can incorporate machine learning and computer vision (the ability for computers to see) to identify locations without human help and feed this data in to mobile navigation systems. Nowadays, with the help of spatial analysis in GIS, computer vision is applied in infrastructure mapping, anomaly detection, and feature extraction by several countries. Specially anomaly detection can be used to identify traffic congestion and road accidents and provide alternative routes to drivers. .

Before releasing any software update, service providers, need to check whether any update could compromise the speed of the system as well. ### 5.4 Research Limitations

The major limitation of this study is, not to be able to collect 385 responses. Only 325 responses were collected, and 12 responses was screened out since those responses had blank answers for the questions related to the variables. And another two responses also were omitted since the participants were below 18 years of age. So, the final dataset contained 311 responses. This limitation occurred mainly because of the current situation in the country where as the main audience of this study, drivers are waiting in fuel queues and frustrated in general. Therefore, asking a frustrated driver to fill out a survey 40 plus questions was impossible after some time. However, author could be able to collect 311 responses thanks to every participant who patiently responded. The current situation of the country is the biggest limitation in every aspect of the research. Daily power cuts, economic collapse and frustrations made a huge impact on this study. In the final survey, people who have not acquired their drivers’ license are not included, however, it could have benefitted more if this category of people are would have been included also. So, lacking diverse representation also considered as a limitation.

The final questionnaire is structured only in English language because of the time limitations of the research and author points out this as a drawback of this research since if the final survey was prepared in all the major three languages (Sinhala, Tamil, and English), more participants would have been drawn to it and more responses could have been obtained and expand the participant circle to a wide area as well. The lengthiness of the survey was a big drawback whereas most participants were very hesitant to fill a 47 questions long survey. Author should have added more subjective independent variables such as sense of direction, awareness, navigation application affinity and attitude and objective variables such as age, gender, the years of experience in driving and the years of usage in mobile navigation systems. And more demographic questions should have been included to get a sense of the audience participated. These two drawbacks occurred because of the limited time frame provided to conduct the research, hence, limited time frame also considered as limitation. Moreover, limitations mentioned in chapter 1.6 are also considered as overall limitations of the research.


### 5.5 Future Research

In first hand, future studies, should eliminate the above limitations, so more accurate and invisible findings of this research are identified. In future, researchers, can carry out a more profound research by incorporating quantitative approach with qualitative approach where, experts’ opinions and perceptions are considered.

This research’s data sample has been chosen both randomly and in convenience of collecting data easily, since random sampling is nearly impossible with current situation of the country, Hence choosing a stratified sampling mechanism would serve better in generalizing the findings with in-depth research. Future studies could include more objective variables in study in pursue of developing a statistical regression model which could provide an actual value to acceptance rate.

Participants characteristics weren’t considered in this research. As shown in some previous studies, the characteristics of participants can affect the results of user acceptance studies. Therefore, future studies can collect more information about the participants. And author believes if the future studies include more representation from rural areas and non-drivers, the outcomes of future research would be more inclusive. Adding mediate and moderate variables to the existing model also could be an improvement in data analysis. Moreover, carrying out a longitudinal research over a long time period may assist future studies to grasp the driver perception changes over time, with disruptions in the navigation industry. The main purpose of this study is to mitigate road accidents in Sri Lanka through increasing user acceptance in mobile navigation systems. But future researches can find more alternative ways to mitigate road accidents in Sri Lanka. Finally, some more points to follow in future research are included in chapter 1.6. Those are also applicable and could be followed.
