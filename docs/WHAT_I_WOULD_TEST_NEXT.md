# Questions I would take into a follow-up study

The original work gave me a view of how surveyed drivers perceived mobile navigation systems. I would not treat that as the final answer for drivers across Sri Lanka.

**Are trust and satisfaction measuring separate experiences?**

They were strongly associated with acceptance in the 2022 study. Before suggesting that a service provider can independently improve either one, I would go back to the item wording, examine how the measures overlap, and look at collinearity and measurement validity. Good internal consistency alone is insufficient.

**Would the same relationships appear outside the surveyed population?**

My original scope and sampling approach limit how far the conclusions can be extended. I would design a new sample that makes geographic coverage, driving experience and digital familiarity explicit, rather than treating responses from one group as representative of every driver.

**What happens after someone says they accept the technology?**

Acceptance on a questionnaire is not necessarily actual use. I would want to measure usage over time, with proper consent and safeguards, and see whether confidence and satisfaction correspond to what drivers do.

**Does that use improve safety?**

This is the most difficult question and the one that motivated the original topic. It requires observed driving outcomes, exposure measures, suitable comparison groups and care with confounding factors. None of these outcomes was measured in my BSc survey. It would be misleading to treat the reported regression as an answer.

I would also make missing-data rules, statistical thresholds and uncertainty reporting explicit before collecting new observations. For any predictive aim, the new study would need an independent evaluation sample.

These are **research directions**, not experiments or results completed in the 2022 study or the 2026 public demonstration.
