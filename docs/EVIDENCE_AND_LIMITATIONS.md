# Where the evidence comes from, and where it stops

**Original title:** *An Explorative Study on Driver Acceptance of Mobile Navigation Systems to Mitigate Road Accidents in Sri Lanka*. Submitted on **12 June 2022** for the BSc (Hons) Business Information Systems at Cardiff Metropolitan University. The assessed cover shows the author as *Hussain Fareed Mohamed Fawaz*. The same person uses *Mohamed Fawaz Hussain Fareed* professionally.

## Included here

The repository contains selected **aggregate values already reported in the 2022 dissertation**, concise explanatory notes and a synthetic Python demonstration. It does **not** contain original respondent records.

## Kept private

The original unredacted thesis, survey response files, questionnaire email identifiers, SPSS working files, saved outputs and syntax are not posted. They require individual copyright, consent, confidentiality and ethics review before public release. Later newly computed findings based on restricted original source records remain outside this release pending that review.

## A discrepancy I have not tried to conceal

The original text says **326** initial responses in one place; another passage says **325**, followed by 12 blank and two under-age exclusions, leaving **311**. The final statistical tables use **311**. The difference in the initial counts has not been reconciled from a verified public processing record. Nothing has been silently changed in the original 2022 thesis.

## What cannot be concluded

- This convenience/cross-sectional survey cannot automatically represent every Sri Lankan driver.
- Statistical association does not establish a causal relationship between navigation features and acceptance.
- Trust, satisfaction and usefulness may overlap; the coefficients are conditional associations.
- Original R² = .679 is fitted-sample variation explained, not an independent test.
- Acceptance was measured, but road crashes, real-world driving and accident prevention were not directly observed.
- No peer-reviewed publication or deployable safety system is claimed.

## Three evidence layers

**2022 assessed work:** original questionnaire, SPSS and historical reported results.

**Public 2026 example:** independently written Python with generated, artificial records. It is a *teaching and inspection tool* and **not** the 2022 experiment.

**Restricted post-BSc research audit:** held in the private evidence archive pending proper release decisions; not represented here as a public or peer-reviewed result.
